# Three-Year Learning Pathway

This is a project planning pathway, not a replacement for a school's scheme of work. The Junior Cycle Science specification does not require one national teaching order. The purpose here is to provide a coherent route through prerequisite knowledge, recurring Nature of Science skills, retrieval and increasing independence.

## Design principles

The pathway should:

- revisit Nature of Science every term rather than teaching it once;
- move from concrete observation and measurement toward models and abstract explanation;
- interleave strands so students learn that science is connected;
- establish practical routines early;
- revisit important concepts through cumulative retrieval;
- build research, graphing, source-evaluation and project skills gradually;
- increase unfamiliar-context and cross-strand demand over three years;
- keep the destination common while varying support and challenge.

- safety and risk;

### Investigation/project independence

Core source:

- `cba-projects.ptx`;
- advanced `investigations.ptx`;
- practical bank chosen to address learner gaps.

Support should fade:

**worked example → partial scaffold → checklist only → independent planning and evaluation**.

### Cumulative retrieval and exam-mode preparation

Core source:

- `interactive-checks.ptx` for diagnosis;
- `retrieval-assessment.ptx` for spaced mixed recall/application;
- `exam-practice.ptx` for original unfamiliar-context questions.

Progression:

1. retrieve definitions and models;
2. explain mechanisms;
3. calculate and interpret;
4. analyse tables/graphs/diagrams;
5. plan/evaluate investigations;
6. transfer across strands;
7. answer in exam mode with supports removed.

## Retrieval rhythm across the three years

A useful default pattern is:

- lesson start: 3–5 retrieval prompts from current + older learning;
- weekly: one mixed question crossing at least two recent topics;
- monthly: short cumulative check including older strands;
- after each major block: revisit prerequisite misconceptions before moving on;
- third year: increasingly mixed and unfamiliar contexts rather than isolated chapter tests only.

## Department adaptation

Schools may teach topics in a different sequence because of staffing, laboratory availability, local fieldwork opportunities, common assessments or departmental planning. Preserve the dependency logic even when the calendar changes:

**prerequisite → phenomenon → model/explanation → guided practice → investigation/data → formative check → application → retrieval later**.

The route can change. The scientific destination and explicit learning-outcome coverage should not disappear.
