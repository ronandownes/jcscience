# Junior Cycle Framework Alignment

This project is a Junior Cycle Science learning resource, not merely a collection of science facts. Its design should therefore reflect the wider Junior Cycle environment as well as the subject specification.

The official Framework for Junior Cycle and Curriculum Online remain the authorities. This document records project implications rather than replacing those sources.

## Outcomes-based design

Junior Cycle Science learning outcomes describe what students should be able to demonstrate by the end of the three-year course. They are not a chapter checklist to be completed once and abandoned.

Project implication:

- revisit important outcomes across contexts;
- use retrieval and transfer rather than one-off coverage;
- keep Nature of Science outcomes active throughout contextual strands;
- show progression from supported first encounters toward independent explanation, investigation and evaluation.

## Common-level science

Junior Cycle Science is offered at common level. Differentiation should therefore come through access, support, representation, depth, independence and challenge rather than through permanently labelled separate courses.

This directly supports the project's three reading routes:

- Read less;
- Standard;
- Go deeper.

These routes must never become fixed learner labels.

## Key skills

Science can develop all eight Junior Cycle key skills. The book should make this real through learning activity rather than decorative icons.

### Being creative

- generate investigable questions;
- compare possible methods;
- design models and devices;
- propose explanations and test alternatives.

### Being literate

- use scientific terminology precisely;
- distinguish evidence, inference and claim;
- interpret scientific texts;
- explain mechanisms and justify conclusions.

### Being numerate

- measure;
- estimate;
- use units;
- calculate physical quantities;
- interpret tables and graphs;
- reason with scale, ratio, uncertainty and change.

### Communicating

- present investigations;
- choose representations for audience and purpose;
- explain data and scientific arguments;
- use diagrams, graphs, tables and prose together.

### Managing information and thinking

- evaluate sources;
- organise data;
- identify patterns;
- compare models;
- test hypotheses;
- distinguish correlation from causation;
- reason under uncertainty.

### Managing myself

- plan investigations;
- select supports deliberately;
- use retrieval rather than passive rereading;
- monitor errors and misconceptions;
- review and improve work.

### Staying well

- work safely in laboratory and field settings;
- understand health evidence;
- make informed decisions about nutrition, lifestyle, environment and risk;
- recognise that scientific learning can involve uncertainty without requiring fear or false certainty.

### Working with others

- plan investigations collaboratively;
- share roles and equipment responsibly;
- critique ideas rather than people;
- combine evidence;
- communicate findings and resolve disagreements through reasons and data.

## Statements of Learning particularly visible in Science

Curriculum Online explicitly connects Science with central Junior Cycle Statements of Learning, including sustainability and understanding environmental, social and economic aspects of the world.

Project implication:

- sustainability must appear across Chemical, Biological, Physical and Earth/Space contexts rather than in one final chapter;
- science-in-society questions should require evidence-informed judgement;
- learners should encounter trade-offs, systems and unintended consequences;
- environmental claims should use data and mechanisms rather than moral slogans.

## Literacy and numeracy

Literacy in science includes technical vocabulary, explanation, argument, source evaluation and multimodal representation. Numeracy includes measurement, units, estimation, proportional reasoning, graph interpretation and quantitative models.

Project implication:

- do not remove technical words merely to simplify reading;
- teach vocabulary explicitly;
- connect words to diagrams, data and equations;
- use unit reasoning as part of meaning, not as punctuation after a number;
- expose common graph and scale misconceptions.

## Assessment environment

Junior Cycle combines classroom assessment with a final state examination and a broader Junior Cycle Profile of Achievement environment.

The book should therefore support:

- learning-oriented formative checks;
- investigation and research skills;
- communication of scientific findings;
- reflection and evaluation;
- cumulative exam-style application;
- unfamiliar-context reasoning;
- removal of scaffolds as independence develops.

The resource should not become an exam-cramming site that neglects inquiry, nor an inquiry-only site that leaves students underprepared for formal assessment.

## Accessibility and inclusion

The Framework applies across a wide range of learners. The project should be accessible while retaining a rigorous scientific destination.

Use:

- optional reading-load reduction;
- audio/read-aloud hooks;
- clear layout;
- explicit vocabulary;
- multiple representations;
- scaffolded practical instructions;
- prerequisite refreshers;
- graduated hints;
- keyboard-accessible interactions;
- meaningful alternative text;
- challenge that is independent of reading load.

## Design test

For any major feature ask:

> Does this help a Junior Cycle learner build knowledge, scientific habits of mind, key skills, independence and evidence-based judgement — or is it merely reproducing the appearance of a school textbook?

Prefer the former.
